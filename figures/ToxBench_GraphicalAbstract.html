The finding

Hold the scaffolds out and every model loses 0.057 to 0.079 AUROC.

On Tox21, a mean drop of 0.070. Random splits report a score the model cannot reproduce on new chemistry.

Random Forest 0.057
XGBoost 0.079
MLP 0.068
GNN 0.075
Random split Scaffold split macro-AUROC, mean of 5 seeds, axis 0.60 to 0.85

It does not generalise, and that is the result

SIDER drops 0.031 to 0.035 for three of four models, the GNN reversing. ClinTox reverses for all four, but with 1,379 compounds, extreme class imbalance and seed-to-seed spread reaching ±0.16 AUROC, that ordering is sampling noise rather than structure.

Scaffold splitting is not leakage-free

Test compounds with a near training neighbour, Tanimoto above 0.6:

43.9% → 12.1%

Random to scaffold, on Tox21. It does not reach zero, so ToxBench measures the residual and ships a filter to flag it.

What the audit adds beyond accuracy

Calibration

Post-hoc calibration removes 67 to 68% of the expected calibration error on ClinTox. Tox21 random forests are already well calibrated at ECE 0.018 and gain nothing from correction. Scaffold splitting worsens calibration on Tox21 and ClinTox.

Uncertainty and applicability

Reliability rises with nearest-neighbour similarity across all three datasets, so the furthest compounds are the least trustworthy. Keeping only the more confident predictions improves AUROC by up to 0.025 on SIDER.
