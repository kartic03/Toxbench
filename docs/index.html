The finding

On Tox21, holding scaffolds out costs every model class 0.057 to 0.079 AUROC, a mean drop of 0.070. Random splits report a score the model cannot reproduce on new chemistry.

The effect is dataset dependent. SIDER drops 0.031 to 0.035 for three of four models, the GNN reversing. ClinTox reverses for all four, but with 1,379 compounds, extreme class imbalance and seed-to-seed spread reaching ±0.16 AUROC, that ordering is dominated by sampling noise rather than by structure.

Why holding scaffolds out is not the end of it

Scaffold splitting substantially reduces train–test analog leakage, but does not eliminate it. ToxBench measures the residual rather than assuming it away.

Residual similarity

The share of Tox21 test compounds with a nearest training neighbour above Tanimoto 0.6 falls from 43.9% under random splitting to 12.1% under scaffold splitting. It does not reach zero, so the benchmark ships an applicability domain filter to flag what remains.

Reliability tracks similarity

Predictive reliability rises with nearest-neighbour similarity across all three datasets. Compounds furthest from the training set show the lowest AUROC under scaffold splitting, which is where an abstention threshold earns its place.

Calibration and uncertainty

Calibration

Post-hoc calibration removes 67 to 68% of the expected calibration error on ClinTox. Tox21 random forests are already well calibrated (ECE 0.018) and gain nothing from correction. Scaffold splitting worsens calibration on Tox21 and ClinTox.

Uncertainty

Ensemble uncertainty over five seeds is usable for triage: keeping only the more confident predictions improves AUROC by up to 0.025 on SIDER. Scaffold-level error analysis shows the aggregate hides wide variation between scaffold groups.
